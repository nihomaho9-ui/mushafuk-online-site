Questions

Short, plain answers about reading, translation, and search. Each sentence stands on its own.

Where should I start?

Pick what you want. Every path opens at al-Fatihah.

  • The meaning in English: Saheeh International, page 1.
  • A short explanation, one verse per page: al-Mukhtasar.
  • A clear, complete tafsir in Arabic: al-Saadi.
  • A tafsir built on reports: Ibn Kathir in English, or the Arabic.
  • The Madinah mushaf itself: page 1.

If this is your first time, open al-Mukhtasar. One verse, one explanation, then the next verse.

A translation is not a tafsir

A translation gives the meaning of the words. A tafsir opens that meaning: what the verse is about, what came before it, and what follows from it.

A translation follows the mushaf page. The Madinah mushaf has 604 pages, so each translation has 604 pages.

A tafsir follows the verse. A complete book has one page for each of the 6,236 verses. The address is the surah and the verse. 2:255 is Ayat al-Kursi.

Should I read a short tafsir or a long one?

  • Short: al-Mukhtasar, al-Muyassar, and Aysar. A few sentences for each verse.
  • Clear and full: al-Saadi. He wrote every verse, in plain language.
  • Reports and hadith: Ibn Kathir. Longer.
  • Large collections of reports: al-Tabari, al-Shawkani, al-Qasimi.
  • The earliest comments: Mujahid and Abd al-Razzaq. They do not cover every verse.

Do not start with a long book. Mukhtasar or Saadi explains the verse in its place. Ibn Kathir is an easy second step.

How do I open the same verse in several books?

The two numbers in the address are the surah and the verse. Ayat al-Kursi is 2 and 255 in every book:

  • al-Mukhtasar in English
  • al-Saadi
  • Ibn Kathir
  • al-Tabari
  • Mujahid

If a book has no page for that verse, the author did not write it. Empty pages are not added.

Which languages can I read in?

The site menu has five languages: Arabic, Azerbaijani, Turkish, English, and Russian. The books go further.

  • Quran translations: 25 languages. English is on t.mushafuk.online. Urdu and Persian are on t1.
  • al-Mukhtasar: 30 languages, including English.
  • al-Saadi is Arabic, with Russian, Turkish, Albanian, Persian, Indonesian, and Urdu.
  • Ibn Kathir is Arabic, with English, Russian, Urdu, and Bengali.
  • The other Arabic tafsirs are on tafsir.mushafuk.online.

Why do some books have fewer pages?

A complete tafsir has 6,236 pages, one for each verse. Al-Sam'ani, Ibn Abi Zamanin, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Uthaymin, al-Shinqiti, Ibn al-Qayyim, and al-Thawri did not write every verse. The five books on t2 are the same. Mujahid has 1,636 pages. Ibn Taymiyyah's Daqa'iq has 312.

Nothing is missing from the copy. The site has as much as the author wrote.

Quran sciences are counted differently. Those pages are chapters, not mushaf pages: 7 books, 726 chapters. The short start is Ibn Taymiyyah's Muqaddima. The long book is al-Burhan.

How many books and pages are in the whole network?

A figure is a published reading page. The same book is counted once.

What is Mushafuk.online?

Mushafuk.online is a free site for reading the Madinah mushaf page by page.

Tafsir, translation, i'rab, and notes on difficult words open from the same page.

There are no ads, and you do not need an account to read.

Which mushaf is this?

The pages follow the Madinah mushaf in the narration of Hafs.

The Quran here has 114 surahs, 604 pages, and 30 ajza.

Page 1 is Surah Al-Fatihah.

Surah Al-Baqarah begins on page 2.

How do I open a page, a surah, or a verse?

Every page has its own address.

In English, the first page is /en/page/1/.

The search box takes a surah name, a verse such as 2:255, or a page number.

Ayat al-Kursi is verse 255 of Al-Baqarah, on page 42 of the mushaf.

How do I change the language?

The language button at the top offers Azerbaijani, Turkish, English, Arabic, and Russian.

That choice changes the menu and the buttons.

The Arabic mushaf itself stays the same in every language.

Which translations are on the site?

The English reading opens with Saheeh International.

Hilali and Khan, and the Rowwad centre, are also there.

Other languages include Azerbaijani (Alikhan Musayev), Turkish (Ali Özek and Rowwad), and Russian (Elmir Kuliev), plus German, Spanish, Persian, French, Hausa, Hindi, Japanese, Kurdish, Kyrgyz, Dutch, Pashto, Portuguese, Somali, Tamil, Tajik, Uyghur, Urdu, and Uzbek.

A translation is a rendering of the meaning, not the Quran itself.

The translator’s name is shown on the page.

What is tafsir, and which books are included?

Tafsir explains the meaning of a verse, the occasion of its revelation, and what follows from it.

The site has 62 tafsirs: 17 in Arabic, 5 early works, and 40 translations. Start with al-Mukhtasar. Al-Saadi is the clear complete text. Ibn Kathir is the one built on reports. Where to start is above. The names and page counts are in the sections below.

What is i'rab?

I'rab shows the grammatical role of the words in a verse.

The books are al-I'rab al-Muyassar, I'rab al-Quran by an-Nahhas, the work of Muhyi al-Din Darwish, and al-Jadwal by Mahmud Safi.

An i'rab page is a different address from the ordinary reading page.

The English i'rab of page 1 is /en/irab/page/1/.

What is gharib al-Quran?

Gharib al-Quran is a short explanation of rare or difficult words.

Two sources are on the site: as-Siraj by Dr Muhammad al-Khudayri, and Tafsir Gharib al-Quran by Ibn Qutaybah.

The note opens beside the word when you tap the verse.

How does recitation work?

The listen button plays a verse and can continue through the surah.

The reciters are Maher Al Muaiqly, Husary, Nasser Al Qatami, Yasser Al Dosari, Muhammad Ayyub, Muhammad Jibreel, and Saud Ash-Shuraim.

The audio is streamed from EveryAyah.

Permission to use the mushaf does not cover these recordings.

How does the on-site search answer?

Search looks only at the Quran text, translations, and tafsirs that are on this site.

A question in English, Azerbaijani, Turkish, or Russian is answered from that language’s translation.

Arabic text is shown when the question is in Arabic, or when a tafsir is asked for.

If one verse fits the question, a direct answer is given.

If several verses are close, cards appear first, and the answer of the card you tap is shown.

Can I share a link to a page?

Each page has its own address, and that address opens the same place again.

Al-Fatihah in English is /en/page/1/.

Translation, i'rab, and rare-word notes each have their own addresses.

Is the site free?

Reading, opening a translation, and listening are free.

There are no ads, and no account is required.

Bookmarks and notes stay on your device.

Where are the sources and the rights explained?

The mushaf, the translations, the tafsir editions, and the audio are not one and the same right.

The Arabic Quran text is from the Tanzil Project.

That Uthmani text matches the Madinah mushaf.

The King Fahd Complex publishes the Madinah mushaf, and Tanzil’s digital text follows the same mushaf.

That permission does not extend to translations, tafsir publications, or recordings.

Each author is named on the Sources page.

Can I read on a phone?

The page fits a phone screen, and a swipe turns to the next page.

The surah list, the ajza, and search open from the top.

Night mode and text size are in the settings.
